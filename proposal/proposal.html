01 · Executive summary

The case for a single operating system

Hotels do not lose money in one place.
They lose it between places.

A night sold on one system, a breakfast posted on another, a rate changed in a third and a refund agreed in a chat: each is defensible on its own. The leak appears at month-end, when the folio, the price list and the audit trail disagree.

LHMS puts the commercial rules and the operational day in one model. The rate catalogue that prices a booking is the one finance audits. The folio the guest sees is the revenue the ledger reports. Every discount, refund, rate change and bulk import above a threshold waits for a named second signature before it can move the books.

The result is not only cleaner accounts. It is a faster desk, a housekeeping board that cannot sell an unfit room, an events calendar that cannot double-book a hall, and a guest who sees the same stay the staff see.

The platform in one line

The operational board and the financial book are the same record, seen by different desks.

Designed for the questions owners ask: what did we earn by department, what is still uncollected, who approved that rate, and what did the night auditor sign?

Revenue assurance

Charges post from the screen that creates them; night audit seals the day and refuses backdated writes.

Margin control

Rate plans, discounts, refunds, FX rates and bulk imports move only through a second signature.

Operational speed

Room release follows task completion; waitlist recovery follows cancellation — automatically.

Guest trust

The mobile portal shows the booked rate, the live folio and the requests the hotel has accepted.

Audit readiness

Every write names its actor, entity and detail; documents carry retention rules.

Group consistency

Each property keeps its own brand, policies and rate list inside one group book.

Outcome

Fewer unbilled nights, a faster month-end, and a single answer to “who agreed this?” — without adding a second system for anyone to reconcile.

03 · Front desk & reservations

Module 01

The desk sees the whole house before the first arrival.

Arrivals, departures, occupancy, balance on house and the exceptions that need a decision — on one board, in the order the desk should work them.

Today queue. VIP arrivals first, then departing balances and in-house requests, with a check-in button on the row.
Room map & timeline. See each key's state and the nights it is free before quoting a date.
Quote at booking. Rate plan, extra adult or bed, early check-in and late check-out priced from the live catalogue; deposit taken at the same door.
One folio. Room nights, dining, spa, laundry and events post to the stay the guest is standing at.
Waitlist recovery. A sold-out date holds a queue; a cancellation offers the freed room to the next matching stay, with an expiring hold.
Departures. Settle the balance, release the room to housekeeping and close the folio without leaving the screen.
Sell the outcome

A receptionist can answer “what is happening in the house right now?” without opening a second system — and a sold-out night stops being a lost sale.

04 · Guest experience

Module 02

The stay in the guest's hand.

A mobile portal that carries the real booking, the real folio and a request that reaches the department that can answer it — without an app download.

Stay without a queue. Dates, room, rate plan and reference, signed in to the guest's own stay.
Requests routed by type. Housekeeping, dining, spa and front desk, each with a service level the board can see.
Self-service booking. Live availability and a quote, including a waitlist path when the dates are sold out.
Loyalty visible and spendable. Points earned on a departure can be redeemed against a live folio.
Profile the house can use. Preferences, allergies and VIP status appear where staff act on them.
Honest surfaces. The mobile-key panel states that the door-lock connector is not live; no guest is promised a door that will not open.
Sell the outcome

Fewer calls to reception, more in-stay spend captured to the folio, and a guest record that gets richer with every stay instead of starting again.

05 · Housekeeping & operations

Module 03

A room cannot be sold while its turnover is open.

The supervisor board, the attendant's list and the desk's availability read the same room state. Completing the task is what releases the key.

Turnover rules, not reminders. The desk's availability follows the board, so an unclean room is not offered.
My rooms. An attendant sees only their list, in the order the supervisor set.
Laundry as a money path. Quote by piece or express rate, move the order through stages, and post the charge to the guest's folio.
Lost & found. A register with status, location and an audit trail — not a notebook at the desk.
Guest requests with SLA. The board flags requests at risk before the guest asks again.
Out-of-order control. Grounded rooms stay grounded; closing a task cannot quietly release them.
Sell the outcome

The most expensive room in the hotel is the one sold dirty or held clean. LHMS removes both errors from the handover between housekeeping and the desk.

06 · Events & banqueting

Module 04

Every hall once, every hour accounted for.

A day schedule that cannot hide a clash, and a quote that carries its package, add-ons and taxes into the guest's folio when the booking is confirmed.

Clash detection. A second booking on the same hall and hours is refused with the booking that holds it named.
Hourly, half-day or full-day. The package is priced from the hall's own list, not a typed figure.
Add-ons and catering. Décor, sound and per-guest catering priced and itemised in the quote.
Hold, then confirm. A hold reserves the time and posts nothing; confirmation opens the lines on the linked folio.
Cancellation terms enforced. The notice window and fee percentage are the hall's, and the void keeps its history.
Pipeline beside the diary. Coming-up events with values, so the sales desk sees what is already sold.
Sell the outcome

Banqueting is where a hotel's best margin and worst reconciliation live together. One quote, one diary and one folio move the margin without the reconciliation.

07 · Revenue & pricing

Module 05

One price catalogue, from the room to the laundry list.

Rooms, halls, restaurants, spa, laundry and event extras are priced from the same catalogue. A change is dated, reasoned, approved and auditable.

Rate plans as rules. Modifier, deposit percentage and cancellation policy per plan — the quote uses them all.
Seasonal lists with windows. A festive list only applies inside its dates; the standard list remains the default.
Bulk export and import. A year of rates in a spreadsheet, validated before it can move the book.
Price history. Every change keeps its old value, actor and reason.
Approval gates. Price moves, discounts, bulk rows, refunds and FX rates wait for a second signature.
Multi-currency. Properties trade in their own currency; the group's base currency is locked once the books hold money.
Sell the outcome

Rate integrity stops depending on who remembers which spreadsheet. The quote, the folio and the price history quote the same number.

08 · Billing & receivables

Module 06

Every charge finds an invoice. Every invoice finds a receipt.

Folios become invoices with a controlled split, payments are recorded against them, and the unmatched cash is visible before the bank statement arrives.

Split without losing the folio. A company and a guest can share one stay's charges on separate invoices.
Payments that settle documents. Cash, transfer and card receipts are applied to the invoice, not typed into a note.
Refunds under approval. A refund is a gated money movement with a reason and a second signature.
Receipts and reconciliation. Bank-matched and unmatched cash are separated, so the desk knows what is still open.
Taxes billed, not hidden. Service charge and VAT appear on the guest document but never inflate departmental revenue.
Receivables visibility. Outstanding balances by guest and company, sorted by what is overdue.
Sell the outcome

Cash is recorded at the moment it is taken. The month-end conversation moves from “did we receive it?” to “what do we do about the balance?”

09 · Financial management

Module 07

The month, explained by department.

Revenue, collections, expenses and margin for the day, the month or the year — drillable to the transaction, and readable by a non-accountant.

Reports that reconcile. Profit and loss, departmental revenue, service revenue, daily cash, outstanding balances, price history and profitability.
Drill, don't guess. A rolled-up figure opens its transactions; a quiet day is shown, not smoothed away.
Expenses with receipts. Recorded against a category and property, approved above the threshold, visible to the approver.
Approval queue. Price changes, discounts, refunds, FX rates and bulk imports held for a named second signer.
Send-back with a reason. A rejected request returns to the requester with the reason on the record.
Role-aware reading. An auditor sees the books and the trail but cannot post against them.
Sell the outcome

Owners get a defensible month at any point in the month, and managers see the financial effect of today's decisions before the close.

10 · Night audit & compliance

Module 08

The day closes once, and cannot be quietly reopened.

A guided night audit confirms each department, packs the day's figures, rolls the business date and seals it. Writes into a sealed day are refused, not reconciled later.

Departmental confirmation. Each revenue centre signs its day before the pack can close.
Business-date seal. A backdated charge or payment is refused with the closed date named.
Exceptions, then close. Unsettled folios and open rooms are shown before the audit is allowed to roll.
Audit trail for every write. Actor, entity, action and a human-readable detail — operational and financial in one log.
Document register. Retention periods per document type, guest visibility and erasure requests recorded rather than silently deleting history.
Night audit, signed. The close is a named act, not an automated job nobody can question.
Sell the outcome

Month-end starts from a closed, signed day. The audit committee's first question — “is this figure final?” — has a date and a name attached to it.

11 · Administration & brand

Module 09

The hotel's rules, the hotel's look.

Policies, permissions, integrations and branding are set per property — so a group can standardise the books without standardising the welcome.

Nineteen permissions, six staff roles. Least privilege by default; an unknown account never lands on Manager.
Commercial policies. Deposit percentage, check-in and check-out times, overbooking allowance and cancellation text per property.
Money of account. Invoice prefix, payment terms, approval threshold, discount limit and rate-age guard.
Integration register. One place to see which connectors — channel manager, payment gateway, locks, accounting, CRM, WhatsApp — are live per property.
Logo-derived theming. The whole interface repaints from the brand, including charts and the guest portal; status colours stay semantic.
Activity and notifications. A group-wide trail and a tray for the exceptions each desk needs to act on.
Sell the outcome

A new property opens with its own identity and the group's controls on day one — not a two-month configuration project in three different tools.

12 · Delivery, security & commercials

Security & deployment

Designed to be run by an operator, not only by its authors.

The API refuses to start in production without a database, a strong signing secret and an explicit origin list. Secrets are never defaulted, and the demonstration dataset cannot be loaded into a production install.

Controls in the delivered build

Identitybcrypt password hashing, signed JWT sessions, rate-limited sign-in.
AuthorisationRole and permission checks on every door; property scoping in the data layer.
Configuration guardProduction boot fails unless DATABASE_URL, a 32+ character secret and a non-wildcard origin are set.
Transport & headersContent-Security-Policy, frame denial, no framework banner; TLS terminated at the proxy.
Operational health/health checks the database, not just the process; graceful shutdown drains connections.
Data boundaryThe web server publishes only the application shell and its assets — never the project root or environment files.
AuditEvery write is attributable; approvals are recorded with actor, reason and time.

Deployment options

Managed: the API and PostgreSQL hosted by the group or a managed platform, the web build served from the same origin.

On-premise: Node.js and PostgreSQL inside the property network, with the same environment guard and backup routine.

Reference architecture. Stateless API behind a load balancer, PostgreSQL with point-in-time recovery, static assets cached at the edge. The health door is what the balancer polls.
What we will not claim. Card acquiring, email and SMS delivery, door-lock hardware and channel-manager connectivity are connectors, not included software. The integration register shows their status; each is scoped and delivered per deployment.
BackupDatabase and uploaded brand assets, restore tested before go-live.
AccessLeast-privilege database user, named staff accounts, no shared manager login.
Change controlAdditive migrations on update; the seed is separated from the schema.

Implementation approach

Delivery

A pilot property, then the group.

Configuration is led by the hotel's own rate list, policies and month-end questions. The first property proves the controls before the second inherits them.

Weeks 1–2

Discovery & data audit

Properties, room types, rate plans, tax rules, departments, roles, chart of accounts and the open items the current systems cannot answer.

Weeks 2–5

Configuration

Properties and rooms, the full price catalogue, policies and thresholds, staff and permissions, branding, document retention.

Weeks 4–7

Data & integration

Guests, companies, open folios and deposits, suppliers, rate history. Connector scope agreed and delivered against the integration register.

Weeks 7–9

Validation & training

Parallel run at one property, control tests (price change, refund, split invoice, night audit), role-based training, sign-off of the seeded book.

Weeks 9–12

Go-live & hypercare

First live night audit supervised, daily check-ins for two weeks, a review of exceptions and a documented handover to the property team.

How success is judged

  • The night audit closes the day without a manual reconciliation spreadsheet.
  • Every refund, discount, price change and FX move has a named second signature.
  • A folio, its invoice and the revenue report agree for a sampled stay.
  • Housekeeping release and desk availability agree on every sampled room.
  • Month-end produces the departmental pack from the system, not from exports.

What we need from the hotel

  • A product owner with authority to sign off policies and rates.
  • One pilot property and a named lead per department.
  • Read access to current rate lists, open balances and document templates.
  • A test gate before go-live where the desk works a parallel day.
Indicative, not contractual. Durations assume one pilot property and a single integration wave; a group rollout is phased after the pilot review.

Scope & commercials

Three ways to buy the same book of record.

Packaging follows the operating job, not a feature count. Pricing is quoted after discovery, when the property count, connectors and migration effort are known.

PackageDesigned forIncludesCommercials
Core OperationsSingle property or a first pilot Front desk, reservations, guest portal, housekeeping, billing, payments, document register. Per property, per month
Commercial & FinanceHotels that need margin control and a clean close Everything in Core, plus events, revenue and pricing, expenses, financial dashboard, reports and the approval queue. Per property, per month
Group EditionMulti-property operators and management companies Everything in Commercial & Finance, plus group rollups, per-property branding and policies, API access and integration support. Group agreement

Professional services

Implementation, configuration and migration are quoted as a fixed scope after discovery. Training and hypercare are included in the implementation plan; additional properties inherit the configured model.

Support covers a named contact, business-hours response and critical-incident handling; the service level is agreed in the order form.

What the price includes

Software subscription, updates, the API, role and property administration, and the integration register. Connector licences and third-party fees (payment gateway, channel manager, messaging) are separate.

No per-user fee for the six staff roles; guest access is included.

Explicitly out of scope

  • Card acquiring, payment gateway contracts and settlement fees.
  • Email, SMS and WhatsApp message delivery.
  • Door-lock hardware, wiring and key-card encoding.
  • Channel-manager and OTA connectivity beyond the integration register.
  • Statutory accounting, tax filing and payroll.
  • Data cleansing beyond the agreed migration mapping.

Commercial terms

This document is a scope and approach proposal, not a price offer. A formal quotation follows discovery and is valid for 60 days. Subscription and services are invoiced separately; taxes are additional.

Decision needed to proceed: the pilot property, the package, and the connector list. Those three answers are enough to quote.

Next steps

From proposal to pilot

Four conversations to a signed pilot.

We recommend proving the controls on one property before a group rollout. The pilot is where the rate catalogue, the folio rules and the night audit either match the hotel's reality or get corrected — with the hotel's own numbers.

  1. Discovery call (60 minutes). Properties, room count, current systems, the reports that matter at month-end, and the first three integration candidates.
  2. Scope and package agreement. Choose Core, Commercial or Group; name the pilot property and its department leads; agree the connector list.
  3. Formal quotation. Subscription, implementation, migration and any connector work, with the assumptions written down.
  4. Pilot order and kick-off. Discovery week starts from the hotel's rate list and open balances; the pilot review decides the group rollout.
What you will see in the pilot: a configured day, a real quote, a split invoice, an approval held for a second signature and a supervised night audit.
